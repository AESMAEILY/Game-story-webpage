Story & lore

Infected with a mind flayer tadpole during an illithid ship's crash over the Sword Coast, a small band of the infected banded together to find a cure before the parasite transforms them, or before it grants them power they can't safely refuse. Their search drags them into a cult's plot to unleash an ancient elder brain beneath Baldur's Gate, with companions, gods, and old betrayals shaping which ending the city gets.

The Nautiloid Crash

The story begins as the player character awakens aboard a mind flayer nautiloid, infected with a psychic tadpole that will eventually transform them into an illithid. Before the mutation can complete, githyanki warriors led by Lae'zel storm the ship, and it is dragged into the Nine Hells and assaulted by devils. Amid the chaos, the protagonist seizes the helm, crash-lands the ship on the Sword Coast, and finds fellow survivors — Shadowheart, Astarion, Gale, and Wyll — who are infected with the same parasite and now bound by a shared need to find a cure before they turn into monsters.

Act 1 — The Emerald Grove and the Absolute

Fleeing the wreckage, the party discovers a mysterious Dream Visitor who appears in their minds — later revealed to be the Emperor, a renegade mind flayer who grants them psychic resistance in exchange for their trust. Their search for a cure leads them to the Emerald Grove, where tiefling refugees and druids are besieged by goblins serving a fanatical cult devoted to a godlike entity called the Absolute. The party can side with the grove's defenders or the goblin raiders, and their choices ripple outward as they explore ruins, the Underdark, and a haunted Gauntlet before pushing toward the cult's stronghold.

Act 2 — The Shadow-Cursed Lands and Moonrise Towers

The party crosses into the Shadow-Cursed Lands, a region blighted by perpetual darkness, and infiltrates Moonrise Towers, the Absolute cult's fortress. There they learn the cult is led by three 'Chosen' — Ketheric Thorm, a general who cannot truly die; his ally Orin the Red, a shapeshifting assassin of the Cult of Bhaal; and Enver Gortash, a schemer maneuvering to seize control of Baldur's Gate. The party uncovers that Ketheric's immortality is tied to Nightsong (Aylin, a fallen aasimar bound in the temple of Shar) and that the Absolute itself is an ancient, captive Elder Brain the trio is using to raise an army of mind-controlled thralls.

Act 3 — Baldur's Gate and the Three Chosen

The party reaches the city of Baldur's Gate, where Gortash has orchestrated his own coronation as Grand Duke while secretly controlling an army of golems, and Orin stalks the streets murdering and impersonating nobles for Bhaal. The companions must root out both threats while navigating the city's guilds, the Steel Watch uprising, and revelations about their own pasts — including the truth that the Emperor once imprisoned a githyanki prince, Orpheus, whose stolen power is the true source of the party's resistance to the parasites.

The Elder Brain and the Netherstones

With Gortash and Orin defeated, the party descends beneath the city to confront Ketheric and the Elder Brain directly. The Elder Brain, having anticipated the party's interference, seizes control of the fight until the Emperor intervenes to save them. The party must then decide whether to free Orpheus and let him take the Emperor's place, allow the Emperor to permanently consume Orpheus's power, or reject both — a choice that determines whether any companions can safely transform into mind flayers to wield the powerful Netherstones in the final battle.

The Final Battle and Multiple Endings

In the climax, the party assaults the Elder Brain and its Netherbrain formation to stop it from psychically dominating the Sword Coast. Baldur's Gate 3 famously supports many different endings shaped by the player's choices throughout the game: the protagonist can destroy the Elder Brain and free the city, seize its power to become a new Absolute and rule as a tyrant, or even side with the villains earlier in the story to reach darker outcomes entirely. Companion fates, romances, and epilogue slides vary enormously by playthrough, and the game deliberately withholds a single 'true' ending in favor of consequences tailored to the choices each player made.
