Story & lore

Once a year, a godlike Paintress paints a number on her monolith, and everyone in the city of Lumière who has reached that age turns to ash and vanishes. Gustave and the sixty-seven other members of Expedition 33 set out from the last generation of survivors to cross the sea to her tower and end the painting for good, knowing full well that expeditions before them never returned.

Lumière and the Gommage

Sixty-seven years before the game begins, a cataclysm called the Fracture cut the city of Lumière off from the rest of the world. Ever since, a mysterious figure known as the Paintress has appeared each year to paint a number on a colossal Monolith, and every citizen whose age matches or exceeds that number instantly vanishes in an event called the Gommage. The number falls by one every year, so the city is dying by inches. In response, Lumière sends an armed 'Expedition' against the Paintress annually, hoping to kill her and end the cycle; every prior expedition has failed. Expedition 33, the newest, sets out on Gommage day itself.

Expedition 33 and the Hunter

Engineer Gustave, with only a year left before he is 'gommaged,' joins the expedition after parting with his ex, Sophie. Most of the party is slaughtered by Renoir, an ageless, armored hunter who stalks the group across the Continent. Gustave survives alongside the mage Lune, the widowed teacher Sciel, and the young, headstrong Maelle, who has been having visions of a masked girl. Aided by the shapeshifting creature Esquie, the survivors press toward the Monolith. Renoir strikes again and mortally wounds Gustave shielding Maelle, but a stranger named Verso intervenes and saves the others. Verso reveals he survived an earlier expedition, that Renoir is his father, and that the masked girl in Maelle's visions is his own sister, Alicia — and that he and Renoir were both granted immortality by the Paintress herself.

Hunting the Axons

After Renoir teleports away his own mansion to keep the party from reaching the being he calls the Curator — the Paintress's 'heart' — Lune proposes forging a weapon capable of killing the Paintress by harvesting power from towering god-like entities called Axons. Joined by the Gestral warrior Monoco, the expedition tears down the Paintress's magical barrier and finally confronts her at the Monolith, killing both her and Renoir in the ensuing battle. They return to Lumière as saviors.

The Curator's Deception

Only Verso knows the truth: the Curator, not the Paintress, was the actual architect of the Gommage — the Paintress had been holding him back all along. With her gone, the Curator turns on Lumière without restraint, wiping out its population in an instant and sparing only Verso.

The Dessendre Family

The story flashes back to early-twentieth-century Paris, where a family of gifted painters — parents Aline and Renoir Dessendre, their children Verso, Clea, and Alicia — could conjure entire living worlds inside their canvases. Lumière itself exists inside a painting made by the son, Verso Dessendre. When a rival faction burned the family's mansion, killing Verso and disfiguring Alicia, a grief-stricken Aline retreated permanently into her dead son's painting, remaking it in her family's image and becoming the Paintress. Renoir tried to drag her back to reality, and their struggle tore open the Fracture. Their remaining son, Clea, created the monstrous Nevrons to help Renoir hunt her down. When Alicia later entered the canvas herself, Aline's overwhelming power shattered her mind and she was reborn without memory — as Maelle.

Two Endings

After apparently dying, Maelle recovers her memories as Alicia Dessendre and revives, learning that Verso manipulated the expedition into expelling Aline from the canvas world. She confronts the Curator, revealed as her real father Renoir Dessendre, hoping he'll restore everyone he erased — but seeing that she still clings to the identity of Maelle rather than Alicia, he instead resolves to erase the canvas altogether. Maelle fights back, reviving her allies, and the ensuing battle brings Aline back into the canvas and drives Renoir into retreat. Realizing Maelle intends to stay inside the painted world forever, just as Aline once did, Verso seeks out the last lingering fragment of his own dead soul that still sustains the canvas — and the game branches into two possible endings. In the 'Maelle' ending, the canvas survives; she rebuilds Lumière and revives its people, including Gustave and Sophie, but also resurrects Verso against his wishes, and her own health begins failing from lingering too long inside the painting. In the 'Verso' ending, Verso banishes Maelle from the canvas and convinces his own soul-remnant to let the painting go, erasing the world along with himself and the surviving expedition members — while in the real world, the grieving Dessendre family finally reconciles over Verso's death.
